## IARC Questionnaire Answers

### Violence

| Question | Answer | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Does the game contain violence? | Yes — mild | Units attack enemies and destroy buildings in a stylized medieval setting. |
| Is violence graphic or gory? | No | No blood, dismemberment, or realistic injury. Enemies are defeated with hit-effect animations and removed from the field. |
| Is violence directed at real-world identifiable groups? | No | All combatants are fictional medieval fantasy units (knights, archers, mages, enemy troops). |
| Does the player control the violence directly? | Partial | The player places units and selects spells before battle; real-time combat resolves automatically. |
| Does the game feature destruction of property? | Yes — stylized | Buildings can be damaged or destroyed during raids. Depicted in a cartoonish, non-graphic way. |

## Content Descriptors to Apply

- **Fantasy Violence** — Stylized medieval combat between player units and enemy waves. No blood, gore, or realistic injury depiction.
- *(No additional descriptors required for the current version.)*

---

## Developer Notes

- Unit combat animations are stylized and non-graphic. When a unit is defeated it disappears or plays a brief fall animation; there is no persistent body or blood effect.
- The "attack your own base" feature shows a heatmap of defensive gaps — this is displayed as a color overlay, not graphic imagery.
- Enemy units are generic medieval fantasy archetypes (infantry, cavalry-like) with no real-world ethnic or political connotations.
- When asynchronous PvP ships, the online interaction questions will need to be updated. Interactive features between players (base attacks) do not involve chat, so the rating impact is expected to be minimal.
- No loot boxes or randomized paid rewards are planned. Any future monetization would use the optional cosmetic model (skins, themes) with no gameplay advantage.
